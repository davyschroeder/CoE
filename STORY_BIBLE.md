# Chronicles of Aether: The Shattered Veil — Story Bible

> **Status: canon reference.** Only the Prologue is implemented in the game so far; everything after it is design canon, not playable content.

---

## 1. Overview & Tone

**Premise.** On the continent of **Elyndra**, the world is forgetting itself. The **Veil**, a network of ancient Anchors that holds physical reality apart from **the Unwritten**, is breaking. Towns vanish overnight. Ruins stand where forests stood the evening before. The dead come back carrying memories of lives they never lived. A bell that fell eighty years ago rings at midnight. The player character, along with three companions chosen from a roster of ten and guided by an in-game Dungeon Master, becomes bonded to the **Heart of Aether**, the engine that rewrote the world three thousand years ago. Every faction on Elyndra wants it, and the Heart holds a truth that makes every choice painful.

**Core themes**
- **Memory and erasure.** Who decides what is remembered? Is a person real if no one remembers them?
- **The price of peace.** The present world is beautiful, and it rests on an old crime. Can it be kept honestly?
- **No clean hands.** Every faction is right about something and wrong about something worse.
- **Grief as motive.** Almost every antagonist acts out of love for someone or something they lost.

**Tone.** Melancholy wonder with warmth. Think lantern light in fog, small-town kindness set against cosmic dread, wry humour around the campfire, and endings that cost something. Horror shows up in uncanny ways, as wrongness, doubled memories and empty chairs, rather than through gore. The world is lived in. Bread is baked, debts are owed, and children play in the square.

**The Dungeon Master's voice.** The in-game DM narrates in second person, present tense. Its voice is warm, observant and slightly wry, and it never mocks the player. It describes the world in concrete senses (smell, sound, texture) and treats choices with respect.

## 2. Cosmology

### The Aether
The **Aether** is an invisible, pervasive energy. Every spell, blessing and pact draws on it. Scholars describe it as "the ink of the world": it carries pattern and intention. Wizards shape it through study, sorcerers through the blood, clerics and paladins through faith (which they read as divine grace), and warlocks through bargains with entities that live in or near the Unwritten.

### The Veil
The **Veil** is not a curtain. It is a structure: a lattice of **Anchors** (buried vaults, sunken spires and crystalline cores) built by the Ithari during the Rewriting. Each Anchor "pins" a region of reality in place, fixing which history is true there. Between them, the Veil stretches like a net and holds back the Unwritten.

### Anchors
- Usually buried beneath sacred or ancient sites. People tended to build temples over them because the ground "hummed."
- Inside, each one is a vault of black stone veined with pale light, carved with Ithari script. At its centre stands a **keystone**, a resonant crystal pillar.
- A damaged Anchor causes **local instability**. A destroyed Anchor lets the Unwritten flood in, and the region gets "edited."
- About **forty major Anchors** are believed to exist. The Heart of Aether is the master Anchor that all the others answer to.

### The Unwritten
The **Unwritten** is the realm of everything that is not, or is no longer, real: erased histories, discarded memories, futures that never came to pass, and the ghosts of kingdoms that were edited out. It is not hell or a dream. It is the margin of the page. Things in the Unwritten long to be read again, and when the Veil thins they **bleed through** as fog, echoes, displaced creatures and doubled memories.

### Resonance (gameplay: Aether Resonance)
**Resonance** is the faint vibration that the Unwritten leaves on the real world. After the Prologue, the Heart's bond lets the player **perceive resonance**, which means they can:
- see the outlines of things that "almost exist" (erased doorways, echo-people, hidden Anchor seams);
- notice when someone's memories don't match reality;
- unlock special dialogue options and hidden paths;
- strike at Unwritten creatures' weak points.

Resonance is both a gift and a mark. Factions with the right instruments can **track** a resonant person.

### Reality Instability
**Reality Instability** is a regional measure of how badly the Veil has frayed (a world-state meter in the game). Its rough stages:
1. **Flicker:** misplaced objects, déjà vu, a stranger who knows your name.
2. **Echo:** grey fog, echo-creatures, doubled memories, minor terrain changes.
3. **Rupture:** buildings or people vanish or are swapped; displaced beings arrive from other realities.
4. **Edit:** whole regions are rewritten; survivors remember both versions.
5. **Unmaking:** reality collapses into the Unwritten entirely. This has happened once.

## 3. Calendar & Timeline

### The Charter Reckoning
Years are counted **YC**, "Year of the Charter." Elyndrans believe year 1 marks the signing of the Ithari Empire's Dawn Charter. **In truth, it marks the completion of the Rewriting.** Dates before it are written **BC** ("Before Charter"), and they are almost entirely mythic.

- **Twelve months of 30 days:** Frostwane, Thawmere, Seedmoon, Bloomtide, Highsun, Goldcrest, Emberlight, Harvestwane, **Lanternmoon**, Mistcall, Deepwinter, Stillnight.
- **The Five Hollow Days** fall between Stillnight and Frostwane. They are unnumbered, ill-omened, and kept indoors. Folk wisdom says "nothing that happens in the Hollow Days is written down." (In truth, they are the seam where the Rewriting stitched the old calendar to the new one.)
- **Festival of Lanterns:** 13–15 Lanternmoon (autumn). Families hang paper lanterns "for those we have forgotten." The custom is older than anyone knows, and it was begun by Ithari survivors who did remember.

### Timeline
| Date | Event |
|---|---|

## 4. Geography

Elyndra is a broad continent. The **Iron Mountains** run along its north, the **Ember Wastes** lie to the south-east, the **Shattered Isles** fringe the western coast, the capital **Solmara** sits in the central river-lands, and **Hollowmere** lies on the eastern frontier.

- **Hollowmere.** A frontier town of timber, slate and stubborn people at the edge of the Whispering Woods: a well-square, the **Lantern & Lamb** inn, Garrick's smithy, Ysolde Quill's shop, the watch house, and the hollow-crowned **Cathedral of Saint Orlen**, whose broken tower has been a landmark for eighty years. It lives on timber, furs and travellers, and is proud of its festival.

- **The Whispering Woods.** Ancient forest east of Hollowmere, where the wind sounds like half-heard conversation. The oldest trees grew from seed that survived the Unmaking and murmur fragments of the First World. Paths move, so woodcutters tie red cord to find their way back. The Verdant Circle's groves hide here.

- **The Ruins of Valdros.** The greatest Ithari city of the Charter era, deep in the Woods: white spires strangled by roots, cracked mosaics, a collapsed observatory. A major Anchor lies beneath its Senate Dome. In Act I it flickers between other Valdroses: one burning, one flooded, one still inhabited.

- **Ashenford.** A smoky river town of glassworks, barges and Covenant tax-houses on the river Sillow. Its Aether-fired kilns caused the Wildfire of 2210 YC. A Covenant garrison holds the town, and the Unbound keep a safe house beneath a cooper's shop in the crowded Low Quarter.

- **Solmara.** The capital: white walls on seven terraces above a broad river, crowned by the **Bastion** of the Iron Covenant. It holds grand temples of the Six Lights, Conclave licensing offices and hospices for the returned dead. Beneath it lies an Anchor the Covenant has secretly walled in iron.

- **The Iron Mountains.** Granite and snow, home to the dwarven clanholds of the **Stone Compact**: deep forges, rune-locked gates, honour-records carved into living rock. Lately whole halls have been found ancient and empty, as though centuries passed overnight.

- **The Ember Wastes.** A desert of red glass, cinder dunes and black pillars, the scar where the First World burned hottest during the Unmaking. Ashkin clans roam it. Aether storms crawl across the glass and leave behind things from other times.

- **Fortress of the Fallen King.** Where the Wastes meet the mountains, a basalt fortress has **risen out of nothing**: Caer Morvaine, last stronghold of the erased kingdom of Morvaine, flying the banners of no known house and garrisoned by the returned dead of a forgotten people.

- **The Sunken Archive.** The drowned great library of the Concord at the bottom of Lake Ithmere, sealed in a bubble of still Aether. Its crystal shelves hold the Scribes' private records, including the only surviving account of the Unmaking. Drowned constructs and archivist-shades guard it.

- **The Shattered Isles.** A western archipelago of broken basalt, whirlpools and coral-white ruins that no history explains. It was the homeland of the erased **Sael**. As the Veil thins, Sael tide-cities rise at low tide and songs drift up from under the water.

- **The City Between Moments.** A city of the First World caught in one frozen heartbeat: rain hangs in the air, a dropped cup never lands, a crowd is frozen mid-cheer. It is entered through a seam in the Forgotten Depths and serves as the Unbound's hidden headquarters. Hours inside may be minutes or days outside.

- **The Forgotten Depths.** A vast underworld beneath central Elyndra where erased things sink: roads to nowhere, houses without doors, statues of unknown heroes, tunnels of drifting memory. Scavenger cults, Unwritten predators and travellers who forgot the way up live here.

- **The Obsidian Citadel.** The Aether Conclave's black glass tower on a sea cliff north-west of Solmara, ringed by floating observatories. It houses Severin Cael's **Resonance Engine** and the Conclave's vault of confiscated Anchor fragments.

- **The Astral Expanse.** A starlit plain of floating stone above Elyndra's sky, reached from the Citadel's summit or the Oradai star-wells. Here constellations are physical things. The stars rearrange because the Oradai's original sky is pushing through. Celestial machines and star-shepherds dwell here.

- **The Broken Kingdoms.** In Act VI, central Elyndra collapses into overlapping realities. Morvaine's fields, Sael waters, Drevakh war-camps and present-day towns all occupy the same ground and fight over it. Here the player glimpses what each ending might look like.

- **The Palace of Echoes.** The innermost chamber of the Heart of Aether, a palace built from memory. Its halls rearrange for whoever walks them, and its mirrors show lives the party never lived. Maelis has kept watch from its throne for three thousand years. The final choice is made here.

## 5. Cultures, Religions & Legends

### Playable Ancestries
- **Humans:** the most numerous people, from Solmara's river-lords to frontier folk like Hollowmere's.
- **Elves:** long-lived (about 600 years), scholarly and ceremonious, mostly in the Silverhollow enclaves north of the Woods. They notice memory slips that shorter-lived peoples miss.
- **Dwarves:** clan-based mountain folk of the Stone Compact who carve honour-records in stone. The Fraying horrifies them, because carved stone now sometimes disagrees with itself.
- **Halflings:** practical, community-minded river and woodland folk, often wardens, millers and guides.
- **Gnomes:** curious, quick and long-lived. Many families run travelling companies of tinkers and players.
- **Half-Elves:** brokers between worlds, at home in Solmara and Ashenford.
- **Veilborn:** born with the Unwritten's touch, usually where the Veil is old and thin. They have **faintly luminous eyes** (silver, violet or pale gold) and dream other people's lives. The Last Dawn calls them "the half-erased."
- **Ashkin:** tall, grey-skinned and ember-eyed. They descend from the **Drevakh Host**, a warrior-people erased in the Rewriting who were only "half-written" back, a people without a history. Having no ancestors to remember, they keep a fierce oral culture of deeds and oaths.

### The Faith of the Six Lights (the Lucent Faith)
Elyndra's dominant religion venerates **six Lights**, divine guardians who "kindled the Dawn" and shaped the world. Temples display seven niches, and **the seventh is always left empty**: "the Silent Light," whom no prayer names.

| Light | Domain | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurel, the Lamp** | Truth, guidance, light in darkness | A hand-lantern |
| **Veyra, the Weaver** | Memory, fate, the dead's peace | A spindle |
| **Thorun, the Anvil** | Craft, home, endurance | A hammer over a hearth |
| **Selenne, the Tide** | Sea, journeys, change | A wave in a circle |
| **Caldris, the Shield** | Justice, law, rightful war | A tower shield |
| **Ilwen, the Seed** | Growth, healing, harvest | A sprouting seed |
| *The Silent Light* | Unknown | An empty niche |

**Saints** are mortals honoured by a Light. **Saint Orlen of the Bell** was a monk of Aurel who, in 2604 YC, rang his chapel bell for nine days and nights through a supernatural fog so that lost people could follow the sound home. The Cathedral of Saint Orlen in Hollowmere was raised over his chapel. Its bell, "Orlen's Voice," was said to drive away fog and evil.

**Hidden truth:** the Six Lights are the deified memories of six of the Seven Scribes (Aurelon, Veyrith, Thoruun, Selenna, Caldrec and Ilwenna). The **Silent Light is Maelis.** Her name was deliberately left out of every record so that no one would try to reach the Heart. Divine magic is real power, but it is Aether drawn through the Anchors, and the faithful "shape" it with the Scribes' remembered intentions.

### Other Beliefs
- **The Verdant Way.** Druids hold that the land has its own memory, older than the gods, and that the Veil is a "scar" the world must heal.
- **Ashkin Oath-Song.** Ashkin worship no gods. They honour **deeds**, recited in long chants at fires. To be "unsung" is the worst fate.
- **Veilborn mysticism.** Some Veilborn speak of "the Choir," a chorus of voices beyond the Veil.

### Legends & Folklore
- **"Lanterns for the Forgotten."** The festival's origin tale says a lantern lit for a forgotten person helps them "find their way back." (Literally true in a small way: in the Unwritten, lanterns are visible.)
- **The Bell That Never Rang.** Hollowmere children say Orlen's bell will ring once more, "when the lost come home."
- **The King Under Ash.** Ember Wastes nomads sing of a king who "lost his kingdom between one breath and the next." (Daerun of Morvaine.)
- **The Singing Tide.** Fishermen of the Isles say that on still nights the sea sings in a language with no words. (The Sael.)
- **The Seven Who Wrote.** A rhyme children chant while skipping rope: *"Seven sat and seven wrote, one stayed down when six went up."* No scholar knows where it came from.
- **The Hollow Days.** Never make promises during the Hollow Days, because "the world won't remember to hold you to them."

## 6. Factions

Each faction has a **reputation track** (Hostile, Wary, Neutral, Trusted, Allied) that affects prices, quest access, patrol behaviour, companion approval and which endings are possible.

### The Aether Conclave
- **Leaders:** **Archon Severin Cael**; **High Seeker Dorran Vex** (enforcer); **Magister Lunet Arvane** (dissident archivist); **Seeker Ysolt Ravencourt** (field agent). **Seat:** the Obsidian Citadel, plus licensing offices in every city.
- **Ideology:** Magic must be governed by those who understand it. The Veil is a machine, so it can be repaired, and improved.
- **Strengths:** The finest mages alive, Anchor-detecting instruments, deep archives, control over spellcaster licensing.
- **Flaws:** Arrogance, secrecy, a readiness to spend "ordinary" lives on theory. Veilborn are treated as specimens.
- **Internal split:** Severin's circle wants to **seize** the Heart. Lunet's faction wants to **seal** the Anchors forever. Vex is loyal only to results.
- **Allies / enemies:** Uneasy partners of the Covenant; bitter enemies of the Unbound; despised by the Circle.
- **Quest themes:** Recovering Anchor fragments, containing outbreaks, ethically uncomfortable experiments, defection plots.
- **Reputation:** *Allied* opens the Citadel archives, Resonance training and Lunet's support. *Hostile* sends Seeker squads tracking your resonance.

### The Iron Covenant
- **Leaders:** **Lord-Marshal Hester Vanbrook**; **Chancellor Ober Lusk**; **Spymaster Wendel Crane**. **Seat:** the Bastion of Solmara.
- **Ideology:** Order is the precondition of mercy. In a world coming apart, central authority keeps people fed and alive.
- **Strengths:** Armies, roads, granaries, courts, and the loyalty of frightened townsfolk.
- **Flaws:** Forced relocations, censorship of "unstable" testimony, Veilborn interned "for their protection."
- **Internal split:** Hester wants the Heart **locked away**. Lusk wants it as a **weapon** to make the Covenant permanent. Some officers quietly aid the Unbound.
- **Allies / enemies:** Allied with the Last Dawn; rivals of the Conclave; hunters of the Unbound.
- **Quest themes:** Evacuations, sieges, spies, hard calls about the greater good.
- **Reputation:** *Allied* brings troops in Act VI and safe passage. *Hostile* brings wanted posters, gate checks and Kael's cover blown.

### The Verdant Circle
- **Leaders:** **The Greenmother, Aunra Vell**, a centuries-old elf druid; **Thorn-Elder Caddoc Briar** of the radical Thornwake. **Seat:** the Heartwood Moot, hidden in the Whispering Woods.
- **Ideology:** The Veil is an unnatural wall that **corrupted** the natural order. The land's memory is older than the Scribes, and it should be allowed to heal even if healing hurts.
- **Strengths:** Shapeshifters, beast-allies, mastery of the wild, the ability to hear the old trees.
- **Flaws:** A cold long view in which towns are weeds.
- **Internal split:** Aunra seeks a gentle reconciliation between the land and the Unwritten. The Thornwake **breaks Anchors** deliberately and lets towns fall to the fog.
- **Allies / enemies:** Friends of halfling woodsteads and Ashkin clans; foes of the Conclave and Covenant loggers.
- **Quest themes:** Healing blighted groves, stopping (or aiding) sabotage, bargaining with spirits.
- **Reputation:** *Allied* makes the shifting paths safe and unlocks the First Tree secret. *Hostile* turns the Woods themselves against you.

### The Order of the Last Dawn
- **Leaders:** **Hierophant Oriel Vantasse**; **Dawnblade-Commander Isaut Merrow** of the militant wing. **Seat:** the Dawnspire monastery outside Solmara.
- **Ideology:** The Veil's collapse is **divine judgment** on a proud world. Repent, prepare, and help the Lights purify it.
- **Strengths:** Fervent followers, healers, refugee kitchens, genuine divine power.
- **Flaws:** Fatalism, persecution of Veilborn and "echoes," and leaders who hide the truth (Vantasse knows the Lights were mortal).
- **Internal split:** The **Penitents** accept judgment and serve the suffering. The **Dawnblades** want to **purge** everything Unwritten by fire.
- **Allies / enemies:** Allied with the Covenant; hostile to Veilborn, warlocks and the Unbound.
- **Quest themes:** Faith in crisis, refugee aid, witch-hunts, relics that are not what they seem.
- **Reputation:** *Allied* grants healing sanctuaries and Seris's full trust. *Hostile* brings Dawnblade ambushes and closed temple doors.

### The Unbound
- **Leaders:** **Odalys Fenn, "the Index"**; field agent **Corvin Thale**. **Seat:** the City Between Moments, plus safe houses in Ashenford and Solmara.
- **Ideology:** History has been stolen. Every erased people deserves to be remembered, and the truth is owed to everyone, whatever it costs.
- **Strengths:** Secret knowledge, forgers, informants, the only maps of the Forgotten Depths.
- **Flaws:** Recklessness at dangerous sites (Corvin's dig caused the Prologue), cult-like devotion to "truth," a habit of using people as tools.
- **Internal split:** The Index wants to **open** the Veil and restore the erased. The scholarly wing wants only to **record**, and is horrified by what restoration would cost.
- **Allies / enemies:** Covertly aided by Lunet Arvane and some Covenant officers; hunted by everyone else.
- **Quest themes:** Record heists, recovering erased artefacts, protecting witnesses, exposing lies.
- **Reputation:** *Allied* opens the City Between Moments, the Depths maps and the Seven Names questline. *Hostile* means your secrets are leaked to rival factions.

## 7. Principal NPCs & Antagonists

### Archon Severin Cael (main antagonist)
A lean, silver-templed human archmage in his fifties who speaks softly and rarely blinks. As a boy in a Solmara plague-quarter, he watched his mother and sisters die while temple healers turned them away. He believes suffering is a **design flaw**. After decoding the Veil with his apprentice Vaelis Thorne, he concluded that reality was written once and can be **written again, correctly**: without plague, war or grief. By Act III he knows the Heart holds the souls of the First World. To him they are beyond saving and could be put to better use. His final plan is to fold **all** living minds into a perfect, painless written world. He is sincere, brilliant, patient and willing to be hated. He never lies to the player. He doesn't need to.

### Seeker Ysolt Ravencourt
A precise, sardonic Conclave field agent, loyal to the institution rather than to Severin. She arrives at the end of the Prologue to claim the Heart.
- **Fought:** she retreats wounded and returns in Act I as a hunter with a grudge.
- **Persuaded:** she lets the party go, becomes a reluctant informant, and may defect to Lunet's faction in Act III.
- **Escaped:** she becomes a recurring pursuer who comes to respect the party.

### Sister Maren Ash (Act I betrayer)
Keeper of the cathedral records: gentle, tired, ink-stained. Her daughter **Elsa** was erased in 3003 YC. No one else remembers her, and no record shows her birth. The Conclave promised to **restore Elsa** with the Heart, so in Act I Maren leads the party into a Conclave trap at Valdros. She is not a villain. She is a mother who took the only hope she was offered.
- **Outcomes:** killed, forgiven or turned. If spared with compassion, her lists of erased names help reconstruct the Seven Names (needed for the hidden ending). Elsa's echo can be found in the Forgotten Depths in Act IV.

### The Bellwarden (Prologue boss)
**Brother Halric Venn**, bell-ringer of an alternate Hollowmere where the tower never fell. He rang Orlen's Voice there for eighty more years. When the Anchor destabilised, he and his bell were displaced into our reality, where both had been lost long ago, and the displacement fused man and bronze into one tolling, grief-maddened creature. Old Wenna remembers him as the kind young monk who let children pull the bell rope.

### Ossaryx, the Gloaming Wyrm (Act I boss)
A vast serpentine dragon from a reality where the sun never rose after the Unmaking and Elyndra became a twilight ocean. It was that world's last sovereign and keeper of light. Dragged through the Valdros Anchor into blinding daylight, it believes our reality is a lie that devoured its own, and it hoards stolen lanterns in a desperate echo of its lost duty. It is terrifying, articulate and grieving. It can be slain, or, through resonance and a very hard persuasion, sent home.

### King Daerun Morvaine, the Fallen King (Act II)
Last king of **Morvaine**, a realm of high meadows and basalt fortresses erased in the Rewriting. He wakes with Caer Morvaine and its dead garrison, remembering a kingdom no map shows. Honourable, proud and devastated, he would tear down the Iron Mountains' Anchor, and the dwarven halls above it, to bring his people back. He can be fought, or made an ally if the party proves Morvaine existed and swears it will be remembered. His crown bears one of the Seven Names.

### Maelis, the Last Scribe
The voice in the player's mind. The youngest of the Seven, an archivist who catalogued the First World as it died, she stayed inside the Heart as its warden and has listened to the souls she imprisoned for three thousand years. Guilt-ridden, lonely and sharp-witted, she sometimes nudges the player toward choices that might **free her** and is sometimes painfully honest. She genuinely wants the player to choose well. Her voice is calm, archaic and fond, and it cracks when she speaks of "the sleepers."

### Other Faction Leaders
- **Odalys Fenn, "the Index" (Unbound).** Blind and Veilborn, she "reads" resonance instead of light. Warm, but a zealot. She sent Corvin to Hollowmere knowing the Heart was there.
- **Corvin Thale (Unbound).** Nervous and earnest, a bookworm in mud-caked boots. His dig destabilised the Hollowmere Anchor and he feels terrible about it. In Act I he becomes the party's Unbound contact, or a Covenant prisoner.
- **Lord-Marshal Hester Vanbrook (Covenant).** Blunt and weary. She evacuated four towns last year and lost two anyway. She fears her own chancellor more than any mage.
- **The Greenmother, Aunra Vell (Circle).** Hears the old trees grieving for the First World, and believes the land is owed restitution.
- **Hierophant Oriel Vantasse (Last Dawn).** Gentle and sincere. He hides the truth about the Lights because he believes faith is all that keeps the frightened from despair.

### Hollowmere Townsfolk (Prologue)
- **Mayor Edda Brannock:** pragmatic and tired; holds the town together with lists.
- **Tobin Marsh:** innkeeper of the Lantern & Lamb; warm, a gossip, rumours and stew.
- **Old Wenna:** hedge-witch, about 88; remembers the bell, and Brother Halric, from her childhood.
- **Captain Roderic Hale:** town watch; gruff, honest, out of his depth.
- **Garrick Dunmore:** blacksmith. **Ysolde Quill:** general goods merchant.
- **Pell:** a boy of about ten who insists he has a sister named **Lira**, whom no one else remembers.

## 8. Companions

The player recruits up to ten companions and travels with **three at a time**. Each has an **approval** track and a three-stage personal quest.

### Vaelis Thorne — Elf Wizard (he)
- **Background:** A 212-year-old former Conclave prodigy who claims he was "expelled for reading the wrong books."
- **Personality:** Brilliant, sarcastic, impatient, secretly lonely, obsessed with forbidden knowledge.
- **Philosophy:** No knowledge is evil; ignorance is the only sin.
- **Ambition:** To read the Unwritten and know everything ever lost. **Fear:** Mediocrity, and becoming a footnote.
- **Secret:** He was **Severin Cael's apprentice** and co-authored the **Cael–Thorne Resonance Theorem**, the basis of Severin's plan and of the Conclave's Heart-tracking. He left out of fear, not principle, and invented the expulsion.
- **Quest:** *Act I*, he recognises Ysolt's instruments as his own design. *Act II*, he steals his notebooks back from the Solmara vaults. *Act III*, the Scribes' account in the Sunken Archive changes him. *Act V*, he confronts Severin and stops or joins him (he joins only with low approval and a losing party).
- **Fates:** The player's archivist (Claim); founder of an open academy (Restore, Hidden); historian of dead magic (Sacrifice); the last reader as the world ends (Restore Original).
- **Relationships:** **Nima** (mentor and rival: he lectures, she calls him a coward); **Seris** (contempt for dogma that slowly becomes respect); **Orren** (desperate to study his seed).

### Brakka Stonevein — Dwarf Fighter (she)
- **Background:** Shieldwarden of Kharn Dovrek. Her home, **Hall Ostrem**, was found one morning as a centuries-old ruin, and her clan insists it fell four hundred years ago.
- **Personality:** Loyal, stubborn, protective, unexpectedly philosophical at the campfire.
- **Philosophy:** A wall is worth only what it keeps safe. Is a person their memories or their deeds?
- **Ambition:** To learn Ostrem's fate and bring its people home or bury them. **Fear:** That she is the one who is wrong.
- **Secret (unknown to her):** She **is** partly an echo from the branch where Ostrem endured. In this reality Brakka Stonevein died as a child, and her grave lies in the clan crypt.
- **Quest:** *Act II*, she confronts the clan records. *Act IV*, she finds Ostrem frozen in the City Between Moments, and then her own grave. *Act VI*, she decides whether she has the right to stay.
- **Fates:** Clan-mother of a rebuilt Ostrem (Restore, Hidden); meets her other self (Merge); fades with a smile (Restore Original); shield-captain (Claim).
- **Relationships:** **Korr** (sparring partners, kindred outcasts); **Kael** (big-sister disapproval that becomes protectiveness); **Tessa** (who is determined to make her laugh).

### Kael Veyran — Human Rogue (he)
- **Background:** A Solmara street thief turned smuggler. He secretly funds the **Wren House** orphanage in Ashenford's Low Quarter.
- **Personality:** Charming, cynical, quick-tongued, secretly compassionate.
- **Philosophy:** The game is rigged, so cheat back, but never cheat anyone smaller than you.
- **Ambition:** To buy the Wren House's deed and get out. **Fear:** Being trusted and failing.
- **Secret:** **Spymaster Wendel Crane** of the Iron Covenant has him reporting on the party, using the orphanage's debts as leverage.
- **Quest:** *Act I*, a raid on the Wren House. *Act II*, summoned to the Bastion, he must confess, double-cross Crane, or betray the party. *Act IV*, he uncovers a plan to "relocate" the Low Quarter.
- **Fates:** Runs the Wren House (most endings); spymaster (Claim); may die covering the retreat from the City Between Moments if his approval is low.
- **Relationships:** **Seris** (law against survival, and a possible romance); **Brakka**; **Varek** (rival dealers in secrets).

### Seris Dawnbound — Human Paladin (she)
- **Background:** A Knight-Oathsworn of the Order of the Last Dawn, raised in its foundling house and sent to judge whether Hollowmere is "tainted."
- **Personality:** Honourable, brave, rigid in judgment, afraid of doubt.
- **Philosophy:** Law is absolute. Mercy without justice is cowardice.
- **Ambition:** To be made Dawnblade. **Fear:** That the Lights are not there.
- **Secret:** Her relic, the **Ember of Aurel**, is an **Ithari Anchor shard**. Her "divine" power is channelled Aether, and her superiors know it.
- **Quest:** *Act I*, does Hollowmere deserve judgment? *Act III*, she learns the Lights were mortal Scribes. *Act IV*, she confronts Vantasse and then reforms the Order, renounces it, or helps bury the truth.
- **Fates:** Leader of a reformed Order (Restore, Hidden); a godless wandering knight (Sacrifice); enforcer or conscience of a new regime (Claim).
- **Relationships:** **Kael**; **Varek** (she believes warlocks are damned); **Ilyra** (sisters in faith who disagree about everything).

### Nima Emberglass — Half-Elf Sorcerer (she)
- **Background:** Her magic awakened in a Veil flicker that burned down her family's Ashenford glassworks and scarred her father.
- **Personality:** Impulsive, excitable, reckless, fascinated by unstable magic.
- **Philosophy:** Rules are walls built by frightened people. Power should be felt, not caged.
- **Ambition:** To master her storm and learn who whispers in the flames. **Fear:** Hurting someone she loves again.
- **Secret:** Her power flows from an **unrealised future** in which she became **the Ember Queen**, who burned half of Elyndra. That future self leaks power, and opinions, into her.
- **Quest:** *Act I*, reconciling (or not) with her father. *Act II*, glass statues of herself in the Ember Wastes. *Act V*, she faces the Ember Queen in the Astral Expanse and must absorb, reject or redeem her.
- **Fates:** A disciplined, wildly creative mage (Restore); revelling in wild magic (Merge); relieved to be powerless (Sacrifice); the Ember Queen herself (Claim, low approval).
- **Relationships:** **Vaelis**; **Orren** (he calms her, she makes him laugh); **Tessa** (best friends and a menace together).

### Orren Mossward — Halfling Ranger (he)
- **Background:** A path-warden of the Whispering Woods like his grandmother before him, loosely sworn to the Verdant Circle.
- **Personality:** Quiet, patient, observant. He prefers diplomacy and stealth.
- **Philosophy:** Everything is balance. Listen before you act; take only what you need.
- **Ambition:** To keep the Woods whole and find his grandmother's "Old Path." **Fear:** That the Woods will become something he cannot read.
- **Secret:** He carries an **acorn from the First Tree**, a living seed from before the Unmaking. The Thornwake would kill for it, and the Conclave would dissect it.
- **Quest:** *Act I*, a Thornwake ambush, and whether to tell the Greenmother. *Act III*, the Sael remember the First Tree. *Act VI*, where to plant it.
- **Fates:** Greenwarden of a forest grown from the seed (Restore, Hidden); plants it in the Palace so the sleepers have a forest (Restore Original); retires to his grandmother's stead.
- **Relationships:** **Nima**; **Korr** (silent respect and shared night watches); **Vaelis**.

### Ilyra Voss — Human Cleric of Veyra (she)
- **Background:** A Solmara hospice priestess who tends the **returned dead**. One of them, **Tamsin**, remembers being her husband, a man she never married.
- **Personality:** Compassionate, gently humorous, troubled in faith.
- **Philosophy:** Compassion before doctrine. Doubt is part of faith.
- **Ambition:** To find where the returned dead belong. **Fear:** That her prayers go nowhere, or to someone cruel.
- **Secret:** She has granted **mercy deaths** to returned dead who begged for them, which the Church calls murder.
- **Quest:** *Act II*, Dawnblades threaten her hospice. *Act III*, she learns Veyra was a Scribe who chose which memories survived. *Act IV*, she finds Tamsin's true life in the Depths and must decide whether to keep him.
- **Fates:** Founds a sanctuary for echoes (Restore, Hidden); lives her other life with Tamsin (Merge); guides the souls home (Restore Original, Sacrifice).
- **Relationships:** **Seris**; **Varek** (she sees his good before he does); **Vaelis** (faith against reason, with unlikely respect).

### Varek Sable — Veilborn Warlock (he)
- **Background:** Born in Ashenford with violet luminous eyes, he was nearly drowned by a Dawnblade mob as a boy. His patron is **the Hollow Choir**, a chorus of voices from beyond the Veil.
- **Personality:** Mysterious, ambitious, dry. He resents needing his patron.
- **Philosophy:** Power is the only protection the forgotten ever get.
- **Ambition:** To become someone who cannot be erased. **Fear:** Becoming one more voice in the Choir.
- **Secret:** The Choir is **the folded souls inside the Heart**, who chose him to carry them toward it. Severin brokered that first contact, and Varek owes him a favour.
- **Quest:** *Act II*, Severin calls in the favour. *Act IV*, he hears individual voices: a child, a Sael fisherman. *Act V*, the Heart's nature recasts his pact as a plea for help.
- **Fates:** Voice of the freed souls (Hidden); welcomed "home" (Restore Original); loses power and light (Sacrifice); the player's dark rival (Claim, low approval).
- **Relationships:** **Seris**; **Kael**; **Ilyra**.

### Tessa Quickthorn — Gnome Bard (she)
- **Background:** Lead player of the **Quickthorn Wandering Company** and collector of "songs with no origin," melodies every culture half-knows.
- **Personality:** Playful, persuasive, socially perceptive, always performing, quietly lonely.
- **Philosophy:** Stories keep people alive. Truth is what we agree to remember, so remember carefully.
- **Ambition:** To compose the **Song of Everything Forgotten**. **Fear:** Silence and being left behind.
- **Secret:** Two years ago on the Shattered Isles she sang a reconstructed **Sael tide-song**. It was a key, and her whole company vanished into the Unwritten. She tells everyone they "disbanded."
- **Quest:** *Act I*, she joins after the party shields her from the Covenant. *Act III*, the Sael tide-cities. *Act IV*, she must sing her frozen company out of the City Between Moments, at a cost.
- **Fates:** Writes the song sung at every future Festival of Lanterns (Restore, Hidden); sails with the returned Sael (Merge); court bard of a new age (Claim).
- **Relationships:** **Brakka**; **Nima**; **Korr** (she writes ballads about him; he hates them and keeps every one).

### Korr Ashmane — Ashkin Barbarian (he)
- **Background:** Former war-leader of the Ashkin band **Cinderfang**, exiled after killing his oath-brother **Thal** in a battle-rage during a caravan raid.
- **Personality:** Proud, loyal, blunt, warm once trusted, weighed down by guilt.
- **Philosophy:** Honour is what you do when no one will ever sing of it.
- **Ambition:** To earn back his name and learn what the Ashkin were. **Fear:** His rage, and that the Drevakh deserved erasure.
- **Secret:** The rage is a **Drevakh echo**. In Act II he learns the Drevakh were erased because they **refused** the Rewriting and fought the Scribes to free the sleepers. The Ashkin are what the Scribes could not fully unwrite.
- **Quest:** *Act II*, a Cinderfang reunion and the Drevakh war-ruins near Caer Morvaine. *Act IV*, Thal's echo in the Depths. *Act VI*, he leads Drevakh echoes in the Broken Kingdoms.
- **Fates:** Sung back into Cinderfang (most endings); a Drevakh war-king reborn (Merge, Restore Original); may die holding the breach in Act VI if no companion stands with him.
- **Relationships:** **Brakka**; **Tessa**; **Orren**.

## 9. The Plot

### Prologue — "The Bell That Never Rang" *(implemented)*
- **Setting:** Hollowmere, 15 Lanternmoon 3011 YC, the last night of the Festival of Lanterns.
- **Opening:** The player arrives or is already in town. They meet Mayor Edda Brannock, Tobin Marsh at the Lantern & Lamb, Sister Maren Ash, Old Wenna, Captain Roderic Hale, Garrick Dunmore and Ysolde Quill, and Pell, who keeps asking whether anyone has seen his sister **Lira**.
- **The bell:** At midnight the **bell of the Cathedral of Saint Orlen rings**, even though its tower was destroyed eighty years ago on the Night of Falling Stone. Grey **fog** rolls in. People vanish, and some townsfolk wake up remembering different families. **Fog hounds** and **hollow echoes** stalk the streets.
- **Investigation:** Through the cathedral, the party finds a **secret way into the undercroft**. Down there they find **Corvin Thale's dig**: tools, notes, the Unbound sigil, and a cracked seal on the Anchor.
- **Lira:** The party meets **the echo of Lira**, Pell's erased sister. The player may **anchor her into reality** (spending Resonance; the anchor draws its weight from the strongest grief nearby — **Sister Maren forgets her daughter Elsa entirely**, which removes her motive to betray the party in Act I), **let her fade gently** (Pell keeps her music box), or — with an arcane caster present — **bottle her echo** in a vial (a forbidden act some companions refuse to accept). This choice ripples into later acts.
- **Echo Sentinel:** An Ithari guardian construct blocks the way.
- **Boss, the Bellwarden:** Brother Halric Venn, fused with his bell. The fight has **two phases**. His tolling **frightens** the party, **resonance pillars** can be shattered to weaken him, and he is **vulnerable to radiant** damage.
- **The Heart:** In the **Anchor vault** beneath the cathedral rests the **Heart of Aether**. Touching it brings **visions of vanished civilisations**: Sael tide-cities, Morvaine's banners, Oradai star-wells. **Maelis** speaks for the first time.
- **Ysolt:** **Seeker Ysolt Ravencourt** arrives to claim the Heart. The party can **fight, persuade, or escape**.
- **Resonance:** At the midnight bell, only the player hears a **second toll** and gains Aether Resonance (glimpses of what places remember). Touching the Heart deepens it.
- **Corvin:** Corvin is found trapped in his own dig and the party decides his fate in the prologue: hand him to Hale, let him go (Unbound sigil), take him as an ally (he opens the Sentinel and the vault), or kill him.
- **Ending:** The Heart **bonds to the player**. Word spreads. Factions begin hunting the party, and Act I begins.
- **Foreshadowing planted:** the lantern custom "for those we have forgotten"; Maren's unusual interest in the Heart; the Unbound sigil; Maelis saying "*they are so many, and they are so quiet*"; Wenna's line "*the bell rang for someone, that night. It always rings for someone.*"

### Act I — "Lanterns for the Forgotten"
*Hollowmere, the Whispering Woods, Ashenford, the ruins of Valdros.*
- **Aftermath:** Hollowmere is shaken. The party chooses whether to hand Corvin to Captain Hale or protect him. (Implementation note: the player chooses three companions at character creation; they are in Hollowmere for their own reasons — see each companion's `bio`. The seven not chosen can be met and recruited across Act I, and swapped at camp.)
- **The Woods:** The Verdant Circle demands an audience. The Greenmother reveals that Anchors are failing across the Woods. The Thornwake is sabotaging them, and the Conclave is "repairing" them in ways that make things worse.
- **Ashenford:** A Covenant garrison town under strain. Kael's Wren House, Nima's family, and Varek, who is found being hunted by a Dawnblade mob.
- **The ruins of Valdros:** Maren guides the party to Valdros's Anchor and claims it can stabilise Hollowmere. **The betrayal:** she has led them into a Conclave ambush, because Severin has promised her Elsa. In the chaos the Anchor ruptures and **Ossaryx, the Gloaming Wyrm** emerges from a twilight reality.
- **Key choices:** Corvin's fate; whether to help the Thornwake or stop them; whether to spare Maren; whether to slay or return Ossaryx.
- **Reveals:** There are many Anchors, and the Heart answers to all of them. Other realities exist alongside this one. Severin Cael personally wants the player.
- **Act ending:** Severin's projection speaks to the player for the first time, courteously, and offers partnership. The party flees toward Solmara.

### Act II — "The Crown Beneath the Ash"
*Solmara, the Iron Mountains, the Ember Wastes, Fortress of the Fallen King.*
- **Solmara:** Political intrigue: the Bastion, Lusk versus Vanbrook, Ilyra's hospice, and Kael's spymaster. The returned dead are flooding the city.
- **Rising conflicts:** Dwarven halls are going ancient overnight, and Ember Wastes storms are worsening. Both trace back to **Caer Morvaine**, which has risen on the border.
- **Allies:** Ilyra (Solmara), Brakka (Iron Mountains) and Korr (Ember Wastes) can be recruited.
- **The Fallen King:** **King Daerun Morvaine** plans to destroy the mountain Anchor to bring Morvaine back, and that would edit the dwarven holds out of existence.
- **Key choices:** Fight Daerun, or prove Morvaine's history and broker a pact. Support Lusk or Vanbrook. Deal with Kael's double life.
- **Reveals:** Whole kingdoms were **erased**. Morvaine was real. The Ashkin are the remnants of the Drevakh. The Covenant hides an Anchor under Solmara. Daerun's crown bears the name of a Scribe, **Caldrec**.
- **Act ending:** Daerun (alive or dead) tells the party: "*Seven wrote us out. Find their library and read your own sentence.*"

### Act III — "The Drowned Library"
*The Sunken Archive, the Shattered Isles.*
- **The Archive:** The party descends into Lake Ithmere and reaches the Concord's archive, then must race the Conclave for its records.
- **The truth of history:** The **Unmaking** and the **Rewriting**, the **Seven Scribes**, and the fact that the Lights are deified Scribes, which brings faith crises for Seris and Ilyra.
- **The Shattered Isles:** The Sael tide-cities rise. Tessa's quest climaxes, and the party meets the echo of **Tide-Singer Ondrae**, the Sael queen, who tells them how her people were "*folded like a letter and put in a drawer*."
- **Ysolt:** She may defect here with Lunet Arvane's dissident faction.
- **Key choices:** Destroy the Archive (to deny it to Severin) or preserve it. Promise the Sael restoration, or not.
- **Reveals:** Severin's full aim, to **rewrite reality "correctly."** Six of the Seven Names are learned. The seventh is missing.
- **Act ending:** The Conclave seizes part of the Archive. Severin activates a prototype rewrite and a Solmara district vanishes. Instability climbs continent-wide.

### Act IV — "The City Between Moments"
*The Forgotten Depths, the City Between Moments.*
- **The Unbound:** The Index invites (or summons) the party into the City Between Moments. Corvin returns, and the Unbound's role in Hollowmere comes to light.
- **The Depths:** The party finds what sank: Elsa's echo (for Maren), Tamsin's true life (Ilyra), Thal's echo (Korr), and the frozen Quickthorn Company (Tessa).
- **Brakka's revelation:** She finds her own grave.
- **The Silent Light:** By piecing together Maren's records, the children's rhyme and the empty niche, the party learns the seventh name, **Maelis**, and that she is the voice in the Heart.
- **Maelis confesses** what she was, but not yet what the Heart holds.
- **Key choices:** Faction alignment hardens. The Unbound's plan to tear the Veil open must be backed or stopped. Seris confronts Vantasse.
- **Act ending:** Dorran Vex breaches the City Between Moments. The stillness shatters and frozen time collapses. The party escapes, though Kael may die covering the retreat if his approval is low.

### Act V — "The Obsidian Citadel"
*The Obsidian Citadel, the Astral Expanse.*
- **The assault:** With allied factions, the party moves on the Conclave's tower. Lunet's dissidents open its gates from within.
- **Personal climaxes:** Vaelis confronts Severin. Varek's favour comes due.
- **The Astral Expanse:** Atop the Citadel, Severin's Resonance Engine reaches into the stars. Nima faces the Ember Queen there, and the party walks the Oradai's sky.
- **THE REVEAL:** Through the Engine, the party sees inside the Heart. **It holds the folded souls and memories of the First World's entire population.** Their sleeping existence **fuels the present reality**. Restoring the First World would unmake this one. Maelis admits she has always known and has always heard them. The Hollow Choir is these sleepers.
- **Severin's answer:** He has known since Act III. His plan is to fold the living too, into a painless written paradise.
- **Key choices:** Whether to spare Severin. Whether to trust Maelis. Each faction now presents its preferred ending.
- **Act ending:** Severin takes partial control of the Veil. Anchors fail en masse, and the Broken Kingdoms spill across central Elyndra.

### Act VI — "The Palace of Echoes"
*The Broken Kingdoms, the Palace of Echoes.*
- **The Broken Kingdoms:** Morvaine's fields overlay Covenant towns, Drevakh echoes march, Sael waters flood valleys. Allied factions and restored peoples (Daerun, the Sael, Cinderfang) fight beside the party according to earlier choices. Korr may hold the breach.
- **The Palace of Echoes:** The party enters the Heart. Its halls show each companion their other life.
- **Final confrontation:** Severin, now calling himself "the Author," must be defeated, or, in the hidden path, persuaded.
- **The choice:** At the throne, Maelis lays the Veil before the player.

## 10. The Endings

### 1. Restore the Veil — "The Mending"
- **Requirements:** Reach the throne with the Heart bond intact.
- **Cost:** The sleepers remain imprisoned. Someone must stay as warden: the player, or Maelis again, who weeps when asked.
- **Companions:** Seris, Brakka and Kael approve. Varek and Korr are bitter. Tessa grieves the Sael.
- **Epilogue:** Stability returns. Hollowmere's tower is rebuilt, but its bell is silent. If Lira was anchored, she stays. The Festival of Lanterns goes on, and few remember why.

### 2. Destroy the Veil — "The Confluence"
- **Requirements:** Shatter the Heart's keystone.
- **Cost:** All realities flow together. Erased peoples return in fragments. Many people meet their doubles, magic runs wild, and borders become meaningless.
- **Companions:** Nima and the Index rejoice. Brakka meets her other self. Hester Vanbrook despairs.
- **Epilogue:** A strange, beautiful, dangerous age of wonders. Morvaine and the Sael return, and Ossaryx's twilight sea washes against Ashenford's docks.

### 3. Restore the Original World — "The First Page"
- **Requirements:** Learn the true nature of the Heart (Act V) and accept Maelis's offer.
- **Cost:** The present world, including most companions, is unmade so that the First World can wake.
- **Companions:** Korr and Varek accept it. Brakka and Vaelis meet their ends with dignity. Seris may try to stop the player.
- **Epilogue:** The First World's people wake in an Elyndra they recognise. A Sael child lights a lantern "for a world we never knew."

### 4. Claim the Veil — "The New Scribe"
- **Requirements:** Defeat Severin and accept authorship.
- **Cost:** The player becomes all-powerful and alone. Benevolent or tyrannical variants depend on reputation and approval.
- **Companions:** Vaelis is tempted. Seris turns against the player if they rule by force. Nima may become the Ember Queen.
- **Epilogue:** A perfect or a perfectly controlled world. The bell rings every midnight, and no one can say why.

### 5. Sacrifice the Heart — "The Long Dusk"
- **Requirements:** Unbind the Heart and release the souls.
- **Cost:** The sleepers pass into true rest, but the Aether fades. Most magic ends, the Veilborn lose their light, and some places return to the Unwritten.
- **Companions:** Ilyra finds peace. Vaelis is heartbroken. Varek is freed of the Choir.
- **Epilogue:** A quieter, poorer, more human world. Lanterns are lit with ordinary flame.

### 6. The Open Page (hidden) — "The Lantern Accord"
- **Requirements:**
  - Spare Maren and recover Elsa's echo.
  - Anchor Lira, or release her with full honours (choose kindness).
  - Return Ossaryx home.
  - Ally Daerun.
  - Learn all Seven Names, the seventh being Maelis.
  - Complete at least six companion personal quests.
  - Reach Trusted or Allied with at least three factions.
  - Persuade Severin rather than kill him.
- **Result:** The Veil becomes a **door**. The sleepers wake and **choose for themselves**: to pass on, to remain, or to be reborn into the present world. The erased peoples are remembered, and magic changes but survives. Maelis is finally released into rest.
- **Cost:** The player's name. The world remembers the deeds but cannot hold the name of the one who rewrote the Veil. Only the companions who stood at the throne remember.
- **Companions:** Almost all approve. Severin weeps.
- **Epilogue:** At the next Festival of Lanterns, Pell (with Lira, if she was anchored) lights a lantern "*for someone we've forgotten*" and smiles at the stranger beside him. That stranger is the player.

## 12. Continuity Rules & Style Guide

### Voice
- **DM narration:** Second person, present tense, sensory and concrete, with warm wit and no snark at the player's expense.
- **NPC dialogue:** Each character has a distinct voice. Townsfolk are plain-spoken, mages talk in clauses, Ashkin speak in short declarative sentences, and Maelis is formal and archaic.
- **Tone:** Lyrical but grounded. Let horror arrive through wrongness, not gore. Every dark beat earns a moment of warmth.

### Naming Conventions
- **Ithari:** Liquid and stately: -is, -on, -ae, -ith (Maelis, Valdros, Aurelon).
- **Dwarves:** Hard consonants and compound clan surnames (Stonevein, Kharn Dovrek).
- **Ashkin:** Short, harsh personal names; surnames from deeds or elements (Korr Ashmane, Thal).
- **Sael:** Soft vowels and tidal sounds (Ondrae, ys-, -ond).
- **Morvaine:** Old-courtly (Daerun, Caer Morvaine).
- **Veilborn:** Ordinary local names; some take "found names" from dreams.
- **Never** borrow names, places, gods or terms from existing fantasy settings or franchises.

### Fixed Canon (never contradict)
- The continent is **Elyndra**. The energy is the **Aether**. The structures are **Anchors**. The realm beyond is **the Unwritten**.
- The **Unmaking** happened about 3,000 years ago, followed by the **Rewriting** by the **Seven Scribes / Concord of Seven** of the **Ithari Empire**.
- The **Heart of Aether** holds the folded souls of the First World. This is revealed only in Act V and must never be stated outright before then.
- **Maelis** is the Last Scribe, warden of the Heart, and the Silent Light. Her identity as the Silent Light is revealed in Act IV.
- The **Night of Falling Stone** was 80 years before the Prologue (2931 YC). The bell was never found.
- The Prologue takes place on **15 Lanternmoon, 3011 YC**, the last night of the Festival of Lanterns.
- **Severin Cael** never lies to the player.
- **Maren Ash** betrays the party in Act I out of love for Elsa, not malice.
- No faction is purely good or evil.
- The player can perceive resonance only **after** bonding with the Heart.
- Companions travel three at a time. The DM is a narrator, not a character in the world.
- Prologue names (Hollowmere, Lantern & Lamb, the Cathedral of Saint Orlen, the Bellwarden, Ysolt Ravencourt, Corvin Thale, Pell and Lira, and the other townsfolk) are fixed exactly as written.
